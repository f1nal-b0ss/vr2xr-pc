vr2xr Guided Onboarding

XREAL One Pro Setup & Calibration Wizard

Follow these 3 quick steps to configure your XREAL One Pro glasses for distortion-free 180° SBS VR playback on PC.

Step 1 • Hardware Follow Mode

Place on Flat Surface & Calibrate

Connect your XREAL One Pro to a USB-C DP Alt-Mode port (or HDMI/USB-C hub). Open the on-glasses X1 OSD menu and set mode to Follow Mode (Stabilization Off) so raw IMU telemetry streams cleanly on 169.254.2.1:52998.

Calibration Progress:
Ready — Stationary bias zeroed
Step 2 • 3D Display 3840×1080

Switch Glasses to Full SBS Mode

Put your XREAL One Pro glasses on. Open the glasses OSD menu and switch Display > 3D Mode > Full SBS (3840×1080) (or hold the Mode/+ button for 2 seconds).

  • Set Windows/Linux display mode to Extend these displays so your PC monitor stays free for controls.
  • Verify that your OS sets the XREAL display resolution to 3840 × 1080 @ 60/90/120Hz.
Step 3 • Zero View Hotkey: Z

Face Forward & Lock Zero View

Look comfortably straight ahead and click Zero View (or press Z). Use your PC mouse on the Touchpad deck or live IMU head tracking to explore the 180° hemisphere.

Keyboard Shortcuts:
[Z] Zero View • [R] Recalibrate • [M] Free-Look
[Space] Play/Pause • [F] Fullscreen
[+/− or Wheel] Zoom • [0] Reset Zoom
[←/→] Seek ±15s • [↑/↓] FOV ±2°
Standalone EXE & USB-C Telemetry

Standalone .EXE Compiler, IMU Bridge & YouTube VR Proxy

Run Build_Standalone_EXE.bat to compile everything into a single vr2xr_PC_Studio.exe file, or run xreal_imu_bridge.py directly.

1. Build a Single Standalone .EXE (or Run via Python)

Double-click Build_Standalone_EXE.bat in the extracted folder to bundle vr2xr_pc_player.html, the XREAL One Pro IMU bridge, and the yt-dlp YouTube VR stream proxy into a single standalone vr2xr_PC_Studio.exe executable!

pip install pyinstaller websockets yt-dlp
Build_Standalone_EXE.bat

Don't have your glasses plugged in right now? Run python xreal_imu_bridge.py --simulate or use the Touchpad & Demo Motion toggle above.

2. Playing YouTube VR180 / 3D Videos & OpenTrack

When xreal_imu_bridge.py (or vr2xr_PC_Studio.exe) is running, it hosts a local YouTube VR proxy on http://127.0.0.1:8766. Paste any YouTube VR link into the player bar and click Load!

Using OpenTrack? Set OpenTrack's Output to UDP over network (port 4242) and run:

python xreal_imu_bridge.py --opentrack-udp 4242
